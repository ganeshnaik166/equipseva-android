**2. CONTEXT AND OCCASION FOR THIS REPRESENTATION**

2.1 The Company is filing this representation pre-emptively and in good faith, in the absence of any enforcement action or show-cause notice, in order to obtain advance classification clarity from the Hon'ble Drugs Controller General (India). The occasion for this representation arises from three converging factors:

2.1.1 First, the global Original Equipment Manufacturer ("OEM") community, both Indian and multinational, has in recent years actively lobbied regulators in the United States, the European Union and Australia to reclassify third-party repair and servicing of medical devices as "remanufacturing" or as a manufacturer-equivalent activity. The United States Food and Drug Administration ("US FDA") expressly engaged with this lobbying effort in its May 2024 Final Guidance titled "Remanufacturing of Medical Devices" (available at fda.gov/medical-devices/quality-and-compliance-medical-devices/remanufacturing-and-servicing-medical-devices) and ultimately preserved a clear conceptual distinction between "servicing" (return to OEM specification, no premarket burden) and "remanufacturing" (treated as a new manufacturer). The Medical Technology Association of Australia has published a position paper urging the Therapeutic Goods Administration to follow OEM-aligned principles. There is a non-trivial risk that comparable representations will be made to this Hon'ble Authority.

2.1.2 Second, the present Indian statutory framework — namely the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 (read with Notification S.O. 648(E) dated 11.02.2020 which brought all medical devices within the definition of "drug" under Section 3(b)(iv)) and the Medical Devices Rules, 2017 — is silent on post-installation third-party servicing. None of Forms MD-3, MD-5, MD-7, MD-9, MD-13, MD-14, MD-15 or MD-42 contemplates a registration or licence for a service-coordination platform.

2.1.3 Third, this regulatory silence creates uncertainty for India's nascent biomedical service ecosystem. The National Health Mission's Biomedical Equipment Management and Maintenance Programme ("BMMP") has itself estimated that between 13 per cent and 34 per cent of installed medical equipment in government hospitals is dysfunctional at any given time, representing approximately Rs. 4,500 to Rs. 6,000 crore of idle public-health infrastructure, and the National Health Systems Resource Centre's "Guidelines on Equipment Maintenance, Calibration and Testing" (2025) explicitly contemplate periodic third-party-style maintenance regimes. A regulatory shift that classified service coordination as "manufacture" would close, rather than expand, this socially valuable channel.

2.2 EquipSeva respectfully approaches this Hon'ble Authority not in an adversarial posture but to seek principled clarity, and to place on record arguments and evidence which may assist this Hon'ble Authority should any future advisory or rulemaking process touch upon third-party servicing of medical devices.

**3. LEGAL GROUND I — SECTION 3(f) D&C ACT REQUIRES "SALE OR DISTRIBUTION" OF THE DEVICE ITSELF; REPAIR-AND-RETURN DOES NOT QUALIFY**

3.1 Section 3(f) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 defines "manufacture" as: "any process or part of a process for making, altering, ornamenting, finishing, packing, labelling, breaking up or otherwise treating or adopting any drug or cosmetic *with a view to its sale or distribution* but does not include the compounding or dispensing of any drug, or the packing of any drug or cosmetic, in the ordinary course of retail business" (emphasis supplied).

3.2 The operative phrase — "with a view to its sale or distribution" — is conjunctive and indispensable. As a matter of plain reading and of settled canons of construction, three legs must be cumulatively present: (i) the activity must be a process applied to a drug or device; (ii) the process must consist of one of the enumerated verbs; and (iii) the end-purpose of the process must be the sale or distribution of the thing processed.

3.3 In the case of a repair-and-return engagement coordinated through EquipSeva's platform, the third leg fails absolutely. The hospital that owns the device retains title throughout. The independent engineer attends the site, diagnoses, replaces a faulty sub-assembly with an OEM-supplied or independently-licensed spare, and returns the very same device to the very same owner. The commercial consideration that changes hands is for the *service* (labour + spare), not for the *device*. There is no transfer of property in the device within the meaning of Section 4 of the Sale of Goods Act, 1930, and consequently no "sale" of the device. The retail-business carve-out at the tail of Section 3(f) — explicitly excluding downstream non-transformative handling — reinforces the legislative intent that activities lacking a sale-of-device leg fall outside.

3.4 The Drugs and Cosmetics Rules, 1945 do not contain any independent definition of "manufacture" in Rule 2; the Act's Section 3(f) is dispositive (a position consistent with the consolidated text of the Act and Rules published by this Hon'ble Authority itself).

**4. LEGAL GROUND II — JUDICIAL PRECEDENT CONSISTENTLY DISTINGUISHES REPAIR FROM "MANUFACTURE"**

4.1 The Hon'ble Supreme Court of India has repeatedly affirmed that "manufacture" requires the emergence of a new and distinct article having a different name, character or use. EquipSeva respectfully invites this Hon'ble Authority's attention to the following:

4.1.1 *Servo-Med Industries Pvt. Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise, Mumbai*, Civil Appeal No. 1071 of 2006 (decided 07.05.2015) — wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court held that sterilisation of bought-out disposable syringes and needles does not amount to manufacture because no new product with a distinct name, character or use emerges. The reasoning is *a fortiori* applicable to repair-and-return service that leaves the device's identity, name, character and intended use entirely unaltered.

4.1.2 *Grasim Industries Ltd. v. Union of India*, Civil Appeal No. 7453 of 2008, 2011 (273) ELT 10 (SC), wherein the Hon'ble Supreme Court squarely held that repair and maintenance activity has "no contribution or effect on the process of manufacturing," and that even physical scrap arising from such activity is not a by-product of manufacture. The judgment is direct authority that repair is a *separate genus* from manufacture.

4.1.3 *M/s Quippo Energy Ltd. v. Commissioner of Central Excise, Ahmedabad-II* (SC) which crystallised the two-pronged transformation-plus-marketability test: a process amounts to manufacture only if it yields a distinct article with a new name, character or use *and* that article is independently marketable. Routine repair fails both prongs.

4.2 EquipSeva acknowledges, in the spirit of candour, the Hon'ble Supreme Court's recent judgment in *INOX Air Products Ltd. v. State of Andhra Pradesh*, 2025 INSC 128 (decided 30.01.2025), which interprets Section 3(f) expansively for the purpose of subsuming "breaking up, altering, packing" within a single manufacturer's licence. We respectfully submit that *INOX Air Products* concerned the scope of an existing manufacturer's licence and the breadth of activities that one licence can authorise; it did *not* hold that downstream third-party servicing of an already-licensed product, by an entity that does not place the product on the market, constitutes "manufacture" requiring a separate licence. The factual and statutory frames are distinguishable.

4.3 The common-law analogue is well-settled across jurisdictions: a watchmaker who replaces a battery is not a "manufacturer of watches"; a service-station mechanic who replaces brake pads is not a "manufacturer of automobiles"; a biomedical engineer who replaces a probe on an ultrasound owned by a hospital is, by the same reasoning, not a "manufacturer of ultrasound equipment".

**5. LEGAL GROUND III — MDR 2017 ARCHITECTURE CONTAINS NO LICENCE FORM FOR SERVICE; NEGATIVE INFERENCE IS DECISIVE**

5.1 The Medical Devices Rules, 2017 — notified vide G.S.R. 78(E) dated 31 January 2017 and brought into force on 1 January 2018 — adopt a finely calibrated licence-form architecture: Forms MD-3/MD-5 (Class A/B manufacture), MD-7/MD-9 (Class C/D manufacture), MD-13 (loan licence), MD-14/MD-15 (import, all classes) and MD-42 (clinical investigation). The framers of MDR 2017 had before them every category of activity touching a medical device — design, manufacture, sterilisation, import, wholesale, clinical trial — and they consciously created licence forms for each. Conspicuously, *no Form contemplates an "after-sales service licence," "biomedical engineer licence," or "service aggregator licence."*

5.2 The settled canon of *expressio unius est exclusio alterius* — particularly when applied to a self-contained subordinate legislation drafted by domain experts — yields the strong negative inference that post-sale repair and servicing was not intended to be a separately regulable activity under MDR 2017. Had the framers intended to regulate it, they would have, and they did not.

5.3 Rule 2(2) of MDR 2017 borrows undefined terms from the parent Act; thus "manufacture" under MDR 2017 inherits the sale-or-distribution gate of Section 3(f) discussed in Ground I above. The industry-standard reading of Rule 2(zk) (defining "manufacturer") tracks responsibility for *design, production, packaging, labelling, sterilisation, installation or processing prior to placing on the market* — none of which describes a coordinating platform or an independent service engineer responding to a hospital's break-fix call on equipment that has already been placed on the Indian market years prior.

5.4 The Medical Devices (Amendment) Rules, 2023, notified on 1 June 2023, made administrative changes confined to state-level testing laboratories (amendments to Rules 18 and 19); they did not broaden the substantive scope of "manufacture" to encompass post-sale servicing. The status quo ante is therefore preserved.

**6. LEGAL GROUND IV — INDIAN INTERMEDIARY LAW, CONSTITUTIONAL PROPORTIONALITY AND COMPARATIVE PRACTICE ALL SUPPORT THE POSITION**

6.1 *Intermediary safe harbour.* Section 79 of the Information Technology Act, 2000 read with the Information Technology (Intermediary Guidelines and Digital Media Ethics Code) Rules, 2021 protects technology platforms that do not initiate transmission, do not select the receiver, and do not modify the underlying transaction. EquipSeva fits squarely within this protection: the engineer is selected by hospital and platform algorithm based on transparent criteria; the actual service is performed by the engineer; the service contract subsists between hospital and engineer (or engineer's principal). EquipSeva neither dispenses clinical advice nor warrants device safety in its own name.

6.2 *Constitutional proportionality.* To classify a software coordination layer as a "manufacturer" without express statutory amendment would offend Article 14 of the Constitution (by treating materially different categories — OEMs and aggregators — identically) and would impose an unreasonable restriction on trade contrary to Article 19(1)(g), unsaved by Article 19(6) because the public-health risk at the point of repair is created by the physical act of repair (regulable directly on the engineer/OEM) and not by the coordination layer.

6.3 *Comparative regulatory practice.*
6.3.1 The US FDA's Final Guidance "Remanufacturing of Medical Devices" (May 2024) preserves a principled servicing/remanufacturing distinction; "servicing" — returning a device to its original-equipment-manufacturer specification — does not trigger premarket or Quality System Regulation burdens.
6.3.2 The European Union Medical Devices Regulation (EU) 2017/745, Article 17, regulates the reprocessing of *single-use devices* — a narrow and specific category not analogous to multi-use capital equipment serviced by EquipSeva's network; Article 2(31) defines "fully refurbishing" as triggering new-manufacturer obligations only where the device's lifetime restarts. Routine repair-and-return is outside both. (EquipSeva respectfully clarifies that Article 18 of EU MDR concerns implant cards and patient information — a different subject matter — and reliance on Article 18 in this regulatory context would be misplaced.)
6.3.3 Closer to home, the Government of India's own Right-to-Repair framework, established by the Department of Consumer Affairs vide committee constituted on 13 July 2022 and the Right-to-Repair Portal launched on 24 December 2022 (National Consumer Rights Day), affirmatively endorses independent repair ecosystems across multiple consumer-product sectors.

6.4 *Indian sectoral analogues.* The legislative response when aggregator platforms emerged in other regulated sectors has invariably been category-creation, not forcible insertion into the wrong licence box. Cab-aggregators (Ola, Uber) are not treated as "motor-vehicle operators" under the Motor Vehicles Act; food-delivery aggregators (Swiggy, Zomato) are not "food business operators" for the restaurant's food under the FSSAI regime; service aggregators (UrbanCompany) are not "beauty parlour" or "plumbing" licensees. EquipSeva respectfully prays for the same principled treatment.

6.5 *Domestic policy precedent.* The Biomedical Equipment Maintenance Certification ("BEMC") scheme, jointly launched on 15 October 2022 by the Andhra Pradesh MedTech Zone, the Association of Indian Medical Device Industry and the Association of Healthcare Providers India, expressly recognises three legitimate biomedical service models: (a) in-house hospital biomedical departments, (b) OEM-operated service, and (c) *third-party maintenance providers*. The voluntary certification under BEMC and the National Health Systems Resource Centre's 2025 Guidelines constitute domestic policy acknowledgement of third-party servicing as a bona-fide regulated occupation.

**7. PROACTIVE COMPLIANCE OFFER**

7.1 Notwithstanding the legal position above, EquipSeva — as a responsible Indian start-up committed to patient safety — offers the following compliance undertakings on a voluntary basis, and respectfully requests this Hon'ble Authority to consider these as the basis of any future light-touch regulatory framework for the sector:

7.1.1 *Engineer empanelment and certification.* Mandatory know-your-engineer ("KYE") onboarding including identity verification, qualification verification (BE/Diploma in Biomedical / Electronics / Instrumentation), OEM training certificates where available, and periodic re-verification.

7.1.2 *Service audit trail.* Tamper-evident digital service logs for every visit — engineer identity, equipment serial number, parts replaced (with batch/lot traceability to the OEM that supplied the spare), pre- and post-service functional test results, and hospital sign-off; logs to be retained for a minimum of five years and made available on request to CDSCO or the State Drug Controller.

7.1.3 *NABH alignment.* Voluntary alignment of EquipSeva's service-quality standards with the relevant chapters of the National Accreditation Board for Hospitals and Healthcare Providers ("NABH") standards for equipment maintenance, and with the National Health Systems Resource Centre Guidelines (2025) on Equipment Maintenance, Calibration and Testing.

7.1.4 *Grievance redressal officer.* Appointment of a dedicated Grievance Redressal Officer with publicly listed contact details, in alignment with Rule 3 of the IT (Intermediary Guidelines) Rules, 2021.

7.1.5 *Annual transparency report.* Publication of an annual transparency report capturing ticket volume, equipment categories serviced, average resolution time, adverse-event reports (if any) and corrective actions.

7.1.6 *Voluntary registration.* Willingness to enrol in a voluntary registry maintained by this Hon'ble Authority (or by a designated agency such as AMTZ under the BEMC scheme), should such a registry be established.

7.1.7 *Scope discipline.* A board-level undertaking that EquipSeva will not, without prior consultation with this Hon'ble Authority, expand its activities into (a) import of refurbished devices, (b) resale of pre-owned devices to third parties, (c) substantive re-engineering that alters performance or intended use, or (d) servicing of Class C or Class D devices.
